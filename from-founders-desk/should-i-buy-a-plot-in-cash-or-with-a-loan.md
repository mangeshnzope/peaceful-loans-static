# Should I Buy a Plot in Cash or With a Loan?

  

  
    ![Mangesh Zope](../../assets/founder.jpeg)
    
      

Mangesh Zope

      

Founder, Peaceful Loans · IIM Calcutta Alumnus

    
  

  
  

A senior tech founder in Bengaluru called us last quarter, mid-decision. He had just received a substantial liquidity event from selling a small stake in his startup — ₹3.5 crore post-tax. He had identified a ₹2.4 crore plot in Sarjapur for a custom family home. The math was simple but the decision wasn't — he could either pay cash and own the plot outright, or take a plot loan and deploy the freed cash elsewhere.

His question to me — *"Mangesh, the gut answer is 'why pay interest if I can avoid it.' But my CFO friend says 'never pay cash for property when interest rates are below your investment returns.' Both can't be right. What's the actual analysis?"*

The honest answer is — both perspectives capture half the truth. The cash vs loan decision for plot purchases depends on your alternative investment returns, tax situation, liquidity preferences, and asset allocation goals. **For HNI customers in 2026, the math frequently favors plot loan over cash purchase, but with important exceptions.** The "avoid debt" intuition is psychologically appealing but financially suboptimal in many situations.

This post is the practical map. The actual financial framework, when each path is right, and how to think about this for your specific situation.

## The Core Financial Question

The decision reduces to a single comparison:

**If your alternative investment returns exceed plot loan rate (after taxes), take the loan.**

**If your alternative investment returns are below plot loan rate, pay cash.**

Sounds simple, but the application requires care.

### The Math With Concrete Numbers

For our Bengaluru founder's ₹2.4 crore plot:

**Option A: Pay Cash**

- Deploy ₹2.4 crore as plot purchase

- ₹1.1 crore remaining liquid for other use

- No EMI commitment

- Plot owned outright

**Option B: Plot Loan**

- ₹1.68 crore plot loan (70% LTV) at 9.0% over 15 years

- Down payment: ₹72 lakh + ~₹40 lakh closing costs = ₹1.12 crore

- ₹2.38 crore remaining liquid for other use

- EMI: ₹1.71 lakh/month for 15 years

- Total interest paid over 15 years: ~₹1.39 crore

**The Comparison:**

- Cash purchase frees ₹1.1 crore liquid

- Plot loan frees ₹2.38 crore liquid (₹1.28 crore more)

- Plot loan cost: ₹1.39 crore in interest over 15 years

Question: Can the additional ₹1.28 crore of liquidity earn more than ₹1.39 crore over 15 years?

For ₹1.28 crore to grow to ₹1.39 crore + maintaining itself over 15 years, it needs to compound at ~5.5% just to break even with loan cost. Anything above that produces net wealth gain.

### Why Equity Returns Matter

For HNI customers, alternative investments typically include diversified equity:

- Indian equity (NIFTY 50): ~12% long-term CAGR

- Indian small/mid-cap equity: ~14-15% CAGR

- Global equity exposure: ~9-11% CAGR

- Mixed portfolio: ~10-12% blended

Even at 10% post-tax (conservative), ₹1.28 crore over 15 years grows to ~₹5.35 crore — vastly more than the ₹1.39 crore loan cost.

**Net wealth gain from plot loan path: approximately ₹3.96 crore vs cash purchase.**

This is the structural reason CFO-types favor leverage over cash for plot purchases when borrowing rates are below investment returns.

## When Plot Loan Beats Cash

Five scenarios where loan is clearly the right answer:

### Scenario 1: You Have Investment Track Record at 10%+ Returns

If your portfolio has consistently delivered 10%+ returns and you can deploy freed-up capital there:

- Loan at 9% vs investment at 10%+ = positive arbitrage

- Long-term compounding amplifies the advantage

- Take the loan, keep investing

### Scenario 2: You're Building Diversified HNI Asset Base

Concentrating ₹2-4 crore in one plot reduces portfolio diversification. Loan preserves liquidity for diversification:

- Indian equity exposure

- Foreign equity exposure

- Bonds and fixed income

- Other real estate (REITs, alternative properties)

### Scenario 3: Construction Phase Coming

Plot loan at purchase time preserves cash for construction phase 18-30 months later. Cash purchase locks all funds in plot, requiring fresh borrowing for construction.

### Scenario 4: Tax Bracket Will Apply Section 24 Benefits

Once construction is complete and plot loan converts to home loan, Section 24/80C kick in:

- ₹2 lakh interest deduction (self-occupied) = ~₹60,000 annual tax saving for 30% bracket

- ₹1.5 lakh principal deduction = ~₹45,000 annual tax saving

- Combined: ~₹1 lakh annual saving for 10-15 years post-construction

This effectively reduces the post-tax cost of the loan by ~₹1.05% in early years.

### Scenario 5: Operational Reasons for Liquidity

Business opportunities, equity investment timing, family medical reserves, children's education funding — situations where flexibility matters more than absolute lowest cost.

## When Cash Beats Plot Loan

Five scenarios where paying cash is right:

### Scenario 1: Conservative Investment Profile

If your alternative for the freed-up cash is:

- Bank fixed deposits: ~6.5%

- Bonds: ~7%

- Conservative debt mutual funds: ~7-8%

Returns below plot loan rate (9%). Loan is more expensive than what alternatives earn. Pay cash.

### Scenario 2: Already Sufficiently Diversified

If your liquid portfolio is already substantially diversified and well-positioned, the marginal benefit of additional liquidity from loan is low. Owning the plot outright simplifies your portfolio.

### Scenario 3: Cash Purchase Opens Better Plot Access

Some sellers offer 5-10% discount for cash buyers (faster, cleaner transactions). For our Bengaluru founder's ₹2.4 crore plot, a 5% cash discount = ₹12 lakh — meaningful adjustment to the comparison.

### Scenario 4: Psychological Preference for Debt-Free Living

Some HNI customers genuinely value being debt-free. The peace of mind has real value even if not strictly financial. Don't take a loan to optimize math while creating ongoing stress.

### Scenario 5: Approaching Retirement

If you're 5-10 years from retirement with this being one of your last major asset purchases, simplification matters. Cash purchase means no EMI obligation in retirement.

## The Tax Lens

Critical to consider:

### Cash Purchase Tax Implications

If your cash came from:

- Sale of equity / mutual funds: capital gains tax (10% LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh; 20% LTCG indexed for property)

- Sale of property: capital gains tax (with Section 54/54F exemptions if reinvested in residential property)

- Bonus / income: already taxed; no additional implication

Cash deployment to plot doesn't create new tax events. But the source of cash may have triggered taxes.

### Plot Loan Tax Implications

During plot phase: no Section 24 benefit (plot loan interest not deductible until construction).

Post-construction: Section 24 (₹2L self-occupied / unlimited let-out) plus Section 80C (₹1.5L principal).

For a 30% bracket borrower with ₹15+ lakh annual interest post-construction: ~₹60K-2L+ annual tax saving on home loan vs cash purchase ownership.

### The Combined View

For HNI customers in 30% tax bracket with sustained income:

- Cash purchase: lose ~₹1 lakh annual tax benefit forever

- Plot loan converted to home loan: get this benefit for 15+ years

- Lifetime tax savings: ₹15-20+ lakh

This pushes plot loan path further ahead in the math.

## How to Decide Concretely

Five questions to ask yourself:

### Question 1: What Are My Realistic Investment Returns?

Look at your last 5-10 years of investment portfolio returns. Be honest:

- Strong investor (10%+ post-tax): plot loan likely better

- Moderate investor (7-9%): close call, other factors decide

- Conservative investor (6-7%): cash purchase better

### Question 2: Will Construction Happen Within 3 Years?

If yes: plot loan preserves cash for construction phase

If no: cash purchase eliminates construction mandate friction

### Question 3: How Stable Is My Income?

Plot loan creates 15-year EMI obligation. If income is stable: workable. If volatile: cash reduces commitment risk.

### Question 4: What's My Tax Bracket?

Higher tax bracket: loan tax benefits more valuable

Lower tax bracket or new tax regime: tax benefit smaller; math less in favor of loan

### Question 5: What's My Liquidity Comfort?

Plot loan preserves liquidity but adds debt. Cash purchase reduces liquidity but eliminates debt. Choose based on your comfort with each tradeoff.

## The Hybrid Approach

Often the right answer isn't pure cash or maximum loan, but a middle path:

### Hybrid 1: Higher Down Payment Plus Smaller Loan

For ₹2.4 crore plot:

- Pay ₹1.2 crore down (50% vs LTV-mandated 30%)

- Take ₹1.2 crore loan vs maximum ₹1.68 crore

- Smaller EMI, less leverage, but still preserves substantial liquidity

### Hybrid 2: Take Loan but Aggressively Prepay

- Take maximum eligible loan

- Aggressively prepay over 5-7 years (target closure well before 15-year tenure)

- Captures Section 24 benefits during loan tenure

- Eliminates debt before retirement years

### Hybrid 3: Cash Purchase Plus LAP Later

- Pay cash for plot purchase (no plot loan)

- Take Loan Against Plot later if needed for construction (we covered this in post #116)

- Different financing structure with different optimization

For HNI customers, hybrid approaches often produce the best balance of optimization and flexibility.

5. **Liquidity preference?** Comfortable with debt for tax/optimization reasons

**All five questions favor plot loan over cash.**

**Expected outcomes over 15 years:**

**Path A (cash purchase):**

- ₹1.10 crore deployed in equity at 12% over 15 years → grows to ~₹6 crore

- Plot owned outright

- Total wealth: plot value (₹6+ crore appreciated) + ₹6 crore equity = ~₹12+ crore

**Path B (plot loan, our recommendation):**

- ₹2.38 crore deployed in equity at 12% over 15 years → grows to ~₹13 crore

- Plus ~₹1 crore home loan principal added through prepayments from equity gains

The "why pay interest if I can avoid it" intuition was psychologically compelling but financially suboptimal for his profile. Tax-aware, return-aware analysis pushed firmly toward plot loan path.

## Peaceful Loans's Advise

The cash vs plot loan decision depends fundamentally on your alternative investment returns. **If your investment returns exceed plot loan rate after taxes, take the loan.** For HNI customers with diversified portfolios at 10%+ returns, plot loans typically beat cash purchases meaningfully — often producing ₹2-5 crore additional wealth over 15 years on ₹2-3 crore plot purchases.

Plot loan beats cash when:

- Your investment returns 10%+ historically

- Construction planned within 3 years (preserves cash for construction)

- You're in 30% tax bracket (Section 24/80C benefits valuable)

- You want diversified asset allocation (plot is one piece)

- Operational liquidity matters

Cash beats loan when:

- Your investment returns are 6-7% (conservative profile)

- Already well-diversified portfolio

- Cash discount available from seller (5-10%)

- Strong psychological preference for debt-free

- Approaching retirement

Hybrid approaches (higher down payment with smaller loan, aggressive prepayment, or cash + LAP later) often produce the best balance for HNI customers.

For young or mid-career HNI customers in growth investment mode, plot loan path captures both real estate appreciation AND equity portfolio compounding — typically adding ₹3-5 crore of additional wealth over 15 years vs cash purchase.
