# NBFC vs Bank for Home Loans: Which One Is Right for You?

  

  
    ![Mangesh Zope](../../assets/founder.jpeg)
    
      

Mangesh Zope

      

Founder, Peaceful Loans · IIM Calcutta Alumnus

    
  

  
  

A senior finance professional came to us last quarter, mid-decision. He had two sanction letters in hand for a ₹2.8 crore home loan. SBI had offered 8.45%. Bajaj Housing Finance had offered 8.95%. The 50 bps gap on a 20-year loan was approximately ₹38 lakh of additional interest.

His question to me was clear. *"Mangesh, why would anyone ever pick the NBFC at 50 bps higher? What am I missing?"*

The honest answer is — for his specific file, nothing. He should have picked SBI. But the question reveals a deeper assumption that costs many borrowers real money. The "banks are always cheaper" rule has many borrowers chase bank loans even when an NBFC would actually fund them faster, more flexibly, and sometimes with fewer effective restrictions.

This post is the practical map. When to pick a bank, when to pick an NBFC or HFC, and how to think through the comparison properly.

## Why They Aren't the Same Product

Banks and NBFCs (Non-Banking Financial Companies) — particularly HFCs (Housing Finance Companies) — both lend against home purchase. But they operate on fundamentally different cost structures and regulatory frameworks. Understanding this is the foundation of choosing well.

**Banks** can accept demand deposits — savings and current accounts — from the public. CASA money costs banks 3-4% to acquire. This gives them a cost-of-funds advantage that lets them offer cheaper loans. Their lending rates are tightly linked to RBI's repo rate via External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR) frameworks.

**NBFCs and HFCs** cannot accept public deposits. They raise capital from banks themselves, debt markets, bonds, and commercial paper. Their cost of funds is structurally higher — typically 1-1.5% above what banks pay. This funding gap is the reason NBFC home loan rates are usually 25-150 bps higher than bank rates.

But the higher cost is balanced by flexibility. NBFCs are not bound by the same internal credit policies banks must follow. They can take risks banks cannot — funding non-standard properties, accepting variable income patterns, working with weaker CIBIL scores — by pricing the additional risk into the rate.

## What the Rate Picture Looks Like in 2026

In April 2026, with RBI repo rate at 5.25%:

**Banks (PSU and private):**

- SBI, BoB, PNB, Union Bank: 7.85% - 8.85%

- HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis: 8.40% - 9.50%

- Kotak, IndusInd: 8.50% - 9.75%

**NBFCs and HFCs:**

- LIC Housing Finance: 8.65% - 9.75%

- Bajaj Housing Finance: 8.50% - 10.50%

- ICICI Home Finance: 8.75% - 10.25%

- Tata Capital HFC: 9.00% - 11.00%

- Aditya Birla HFL: 9.25% - 11.50%

- Smaller NBFCs: 9.50% - 13.50%

Across the spectrum, NBFCs run 25-150 bps above the bank rate for a comparable file. For a ₹2 crore loan over 20 years, this gap translates to ₹15-50 lakh of additional interest paid.

The question worth asking — what does the NBFC give you for that premium?

## What NBFCs Genuinely Do Better

For specific borrower profiles, NBFC flexibility outweighs the rate premium. Five concrete cases:

### Case 1: Self-Employed With Variable Income

NBFCs use what they call "assessed income" methods — they look at bank statements, business turnover, GST returns, and lifestyle indicators rather than just declared ITR income. For a business owner whose actual cash flow is ₹80 lakh annually but ITR shows ₹40 lakh (due to legitimate tax planning), an NBFC can fund based on a calibrated ₹60 lakh "assessed" figure. A bank would fund based on the ₹40 lakh ITR figure alone.

The eligibility uplift on self-employed files is often 30-60% with an NBFC vs a bank. For ₹2 Cr+ home purchases by founders and business owners, this can be the difference between getting the loan and not.

### Case 2: Funded Startup Founders

We covered this in our startup founders post. Banks struggle with the founder profile — modest declared salary, substantial unrealised equity. NBFCs (particularly Bajaj Housing Finance, ICICI HFC) have specific products that recognise the underlying earning capacity and asset position better.

### Case 3: Non-Standard Properties

Banks maintain strict approved-project lists. If your target property isn't on a major bank's list — older buildings, less-documented chain of title, non-RERA projects in states where RERA is mandatory — your file will be rejected by banks. NBFCs are more willing to look at these properties on a case-by-case basis, often with lower LTV (60-65% instead of 75-80%) and higher rate.

### Case 4: CIBIL in the 650-720 Range

If your CIBIL is below 720, most private banks will decline outright. PSU banks may approve with conditions and a meaningful rate premium. NBFCs are comfortable in this zone — they price the risk via 1-2% rate premium and proceed.

For borrowers improving their CIBIL but who need to buy now, an NBFC loan is often the workable path. After 18-24 months of clean repayment, a balance transfer to a bank at the better rate is straightforward.

### Case 5: Speed Matters

Banks typically take 3-6 weeks from application to disbursement. NBFCs can do 5-12 days for clean files. For someone facing a registration deadline or competitive bid timeline, the speed advantage is real.

## What Banks Genuinely Do Better

Five cases where banks are unambiguously the better choice:

### Case 1: Strong Salaried Profile With High CIBIL

If you are salaried at a Cat-A employer, CIBIL above 770, with stable 3+ years of employment, banks will fund you at their best rates. You should not pay an NBFC premium for a file that banks treat as gold standard. The 50-100 bps lower rate compounds into ₹25-60 lakh of saved interest over 20 years on a ₹2 crore loan.

### Case 2: Standard Approved-List Properties

If you are buying a flat in a major project (Hiranandani, Lodha, Godrej, DLF, Prestige, etc.) that all major banks have pre-approved, the property-side risk is low and banks compete aggressively. Take advantage of that competition.

### Case 3: You Want Long-Term Banking Relationship

If your salary, FDs, and investments are with one bank, the home loan creates a deeper relationship that often results in better service over 20 years — premium banking access, faster customer support, easier mid-loan negotiations. NBFCs are typically transactional only.

### Case 4: You Plan to Aggressively Use Repo Rate Cuts

When RBI cuts repo rate, banks pass it through within 90 days due to EBLR framework rigour. NBFCs are less rigorous — some pass cuts through partially, some delay. Over a 20-year loan with multiple rate cycles, banks tend to capture the rate-cut benefits more reliably.

### Case 5: Standard Loan Sizes (₹50 lakh - ₹2 crore)

Banks compete most aggressively in this range. Their automated processes are optimised for it, their pricing is competitive, and the regulatory transparency is highest. For a ₹2 Cr+ HNI loan with a clean salaried profile, banks should be your first stop.

## The Hybrid Strategy Most Borrowers Don't Consider

For files where the bank-vs-NBFC choice is genuinely close, there is a third option — **start with NBFC, transfer to bank later**.

How it works:

1. Take the loan from an NBFC initially (faster approval, more flexible)

2. Build 12-18 months of clean repayment history

3. Once your loan account has matured and your CIBIL has strengthened, do a balance transfer to a bank at the better rate

With RBI's January 2026 directive eliminating prepayment penalties on floating-rate home loans, this hybrid approach is genuinely workable. The cost of switching from NBFC to bank is now mostly the new bank's processing fee (typically 0.5%) and legal/technical charges — negligible compared to the rate savings.

For borrowers whose immediate file fits NBFCs better but whose 18-month profile will fit banks better, this two-step structure delivers the best of both — speed initially, rate optimisation later.

## What to Compare (Beyond the Headline Rate)

When evaluating bank vs NBFC offers, six factors matter beyond the interest rate:

**1. Processing Fee.** Banks: 0.25% - 0.50%. NBFCs: 0.50% - 1.50%. On a ₹2 crore loan, the gap is ₹50,000 - ₹2 lakh.

**2. Legal and Technical Fees.** Usually similar across both, but NBFCs sometimes bundle these into higher amounts.

**3. Foreclosure Charges.** For floating-rate loans post-January 2026, both banks and NBFCs cannot charge foreclosure penalties on individual non-business loans. For fixed-rate loans, both can charge — typically 2-4%.

**4. Conversion Charges.** Banks typically charge ₹5,000-15,000 to convert from one rate structure to another. NBFCs are often more expensive on this.

**5. Customer Service Infrastructure.** Banks have larger physical branch networks, longer customer service histories, and typically better grievance redressal mechanisms. NBFCs vary widely — some are excellent (Bajaj, ICICI HFC), some are poor.

**6. Top-Up Loan Availability.** For accessing additional funds against the same property later (we covered this in our top-up post), bank top-ups are typically available at smaller premiums over the home loan rate. NBFC top-ups exist but at higher premiums.

## A Concrete Decision Framework

Use this simple framework for your specific file:

**Default to a bank if:**

- CIBIL above 750

- Salaried with 2+ years at Cat-A employer

- Property in a bank's approved project list

- Loan size ₹50 lakh - ₹3 crore

- You want the best rate over the loan tenure

**Default to an NBFC/HFC if:**

- Self-employed with assessed-income complexity

- Founder/business owner profile

- Property is non-standard or not on bank's approved list

- CIBIL in 650-720 range

- Speed is genuinely critical (registration deadline)

- You can balance-transfer to a bank in 18 months

**Strongly consider both, then negotiate:**

- For all the in-between profiles. Get sanctions from one bank and one NBFC. Use them as competitive leverage with each other. Rate negotiations move 25-50 bps when you have parallel offers.

## What I Told the Finance Professional

For the borrower I mentioned at the start, the analysis was straightforward:

- CIBIL 810

- Salaried at HDFC Bank, 9 years

- Property in a Lodha tower (on every major bank's approved list)

- Loan size ₹2.8 crore

Every factor pointed to a bank. The 50 bps gap (8.45% vs 8.95%) translated to ₹38 lakh of saved interest over 20 years. There was no flexibility benefit from Bajaj Housing Finance that justified that premium for his specific file.

He took the SBI offer. Six weeks later, used the Bajaj sanction letter as leverage to negotiate the SBI rate down further by 15 bps to 8.30%. Total savings vs the original choice: approximately ₹45 lakh over the loan life.

The NBFC sanction wasn't wasted — it was the negotiation tool that improved the bank's offer. That is a perfectly good use of an NBFC sanction even when you eventually pick the bank.

## Peaceful Loans's Advise

Banks and NBFCs are not interchangeable products competing on rate. They are different products with different strengths, suited to different borrower profiles.

For a strong salaried file with clean property, bank loans win on cost and total tenure economics. For self-employed, founder, atypical property, lower-CIBIL, or speed-critical files, NBFCs offer real flexibility that banks cannot match.

The smartest borrowers don't pick one or the other — they get sanctions from both and let the offers compete. Even when you eventually take the bank loan, the NBFC sanction is a valuable negotiation tool.
