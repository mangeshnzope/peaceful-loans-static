Can I Get an NRI Home Loan While on H-1B Visa?

Mangesh Zope

Founder, Peaceful Loans · IIM Calcutta Alumnus

A senior software engineer in San Jose called us last quarter, anxious. He had been on H-1B for 6 years, his Green Card application was 4-5 years out, and he had heard mixed messaging from friends — "H-1B is risky for home loans, banks are nervous about deportation, you'll get a higher rate, may not even get approved."

His question — "Mangesh, am I going to face problems just because I'm on H-1B? Should I wait until I get my Green Card?"

The honest answer is — H-1B is one of the strongest visa categories for NRI home loans. Not riskiest. Not problematic. Banks see H-1B holders as genuinely premium NRI customers — strong income, professional employer, multi-year track record. The "H-1B risk" narrative your friends mentioned is mostly outdated or misinformed.

This post is the practical map for H-1B holders applying for Indian home loans. What banks actually look for, why H-1B works well, and how to address the few genuine concerns that do exist.

Why H-1B Is Considered "Golden" by Indian Banks

Across the documents and conversations bank credit teams use to evaluate NRI files, H-1B-specific signals are consistently strong:

Signal 1: Stable High Income

H-1B holders typically earn $100,000-$300,000+ in major US tech, finance, and consulting firms. The income translates to strong INR equivalents (₹6-25 lakh/month) supporting substantial loan eligibility.

Signal 2: Premium Employer Profile

H-1B is granted to specialty occupations at qualified employers. The vast majority of H-1B holders work at:

  • Large US tech (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Apple, Oracle, Salesforce, etc.)
  • Top consulting firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, Accenture)
  • Investment banks and hedge funds
  • Established pharma, biotech, and engineering firms

These are virtually all in banks' "Cat A" employer categories — automatic best rate slabs.

Signal 3: 3+ Year Documented US Track Record

H-1B holders have:

  • Multiple years of W-2 forms documented with IRS
  • Form 1040 tax returns
  • US bank statements showing salary deposits
  • US credit history (FICO scores)
  • Employer letters confirming role and tenure

This documentation depth is exactly what banks want for income verification. It's often more comprehensive than what some resident borrowers can produce.

Signal 4: H-1B Validity Periods Are Predictable

H-1B is initially granted for 3 years, extendable for another 3 years (6 total), with further extensions possible while Green Card processing is ongoing. From a bank's perspective:

  • Total validity is typically 6+ years from initial filing
  • Extensions are routine for productive workers
  • Most H-1B holders eventually transition to Green Card or O-1 / EB-5 paths

A 6-year validity is more than enough for banks. They're not underwriting your entire 20-year loan against your visa — they're underwriting your current ability to service and your banking relationship.

Signal 5: Banks Have Specific H-1B Products and Processes

Major Indian banks (HDFC, ICICI, SBI, Axis, Kotak) have specific NRI desks that handle H-1B applications regularly. The process is well-trodden — typically 60-80% of US-based NRI loan applications come from H-1B holders.

What Banks Actually Verify for H-1B Applicants

The H-1B-specific verifications are predictable:

Verification 1: Visa Validity

Banks want at least 18 months of residual visa validity at the time of application. If your H-1B expires in 12 months, banks will be cautious — though if extension is filed and pending, this is usually accommodated.

If your visa is in active extension processing (LCA filed, I-129 pending), document this clearly with the bank — show employer's filing confirmation.

Verification 2: Employer Stability

Banks check:

  • Has your employer filed your H-1B (you're not a contractor on someone else's H-1B)
  • Is your employer financially healthy
  • Has your employment been continuous

For tier-1 tech / consulting / finance employers, this is rarely a concern. For smaller employers or recent layoff exposure, banks may probe more.

Verification 3: I-797 Approval Notice

The official USCIS I-797 approval notice is the document banks ask for. Don't try to substitute with offer letters or W-2 forms — get the I-797 from your immigration attorney or HR.

Verification 4: Tax Compliance

Form 1040 returns with W-2 attached for last 2 years. Some banks ask for IRS transcripts for additional verification. Make sure your tax filings are current and clean.

Verification 5: International Credit Score

US-based NRIs need:

  • FICO score from Experian, Equifax, or TransUnion
  • Credit report not older than 45 days at time of application
  • Score of 750+ for premium rate slabs (most H-1B holders qualify here)

Common Concerns Friends Mention (Mostly Misplaced)

Five myths H-1B holders often hear:

Myth 1: "Banks Won't Lend to H-1B Holders Because of Deportation Risk"

Reality: Banks underwrite based on your current ability to service the loan, not on hypothetical deportation scenarios. If you lose your H-1B for any reason and have to leave the US, you would still owe the loan — and the bank can recover via the property in India regardless of your visa status.

The "deportation risk" concern conflates two different things: the bank's recovery risk (low, since collateral is in India) and your personal risk (real, but separate from the loan question).

Myth 2: "You'll Get a Higher Rate Because of Visa Risk"

Reality: H-1B holders typically get the same rate as Green Card holders or US citizens of Indian origin. The differentiator is your CIBIL/FICO score, employer, and loan size — not your specific visa category within the work-authorisation tiers.

Myth 3: "You Need to Have a Green Card First"

Reality: Green Card is not required. Tens of thousands of H-1B holders have successfully closed Indian home loans. Some major NRI lender stats suggest 60-70% of US-based NRI loans come from H-1B holders, not Green Card holders.

Myth 4: "You Need a Co-Applicant Because of Visa Status"

Reality: Many H-1B holders close loans solo without any co-applicant. Adding a resident-Indian co-applicant (spouse, parent) can increase eligibility, but it's not required for visa-status reasons.

Myth 5: "The Process Is Especially Hard for H-1B"

Reality: The process is the same as any US-based NRI. The 12-22 week timeline applies to H-1B holders the same as Green Card holders. Documentation is identical.

When H-1B Status Genuinely Creates Concerns

Three situations where H-1B status legitimately complicates the loan:

Concern 1: Recent Job Loss / Layoff

If you've recently been laid off (within last 6 months), banks will be cautious — not because of H-1B specifically, but because employment continuity is uncertain. The 60-day grace period after H-1B termination is real and visible to underwriters.

If you're in this situation, wait until you've stabilised at a new employer for at least 3-6 months before applying.

Concern 2: H-1B Transfer Mid-Process

If you're in the middle of an H-1B transfer (changing employers), banks will want to wait until the new employment is stable. The transfer itself isn't a problem; the timing relative to your loan application is.

Concern 3: Visa Expiring Within 12 Months Without Extension Filed

If your H-1B expires within 12 months and your employer hasn't filed an extension, this raises real questions about employment continuity. Get the extension filed before applying.

How to Strengthen Your H-1B File

Six concrete actions:

Action 1: Document Your H-1B Status Cleanly

Keep handy:

  • I-797 approval notice (current)
  • LCA (Labor Condition Application) for current role
  • Employer letter confirming current employment, role, and tenure
  • Any extension/transfer documentation if relevant

A clean documentation pack demonstrates to underwriters that the visa side is sorted.

Action 2: Show Multi-Year US Income History

3+ years of W-2 forms + Form 1040 + US bank statements showing salary credits demonstrate stable, growing US earnings. This is the strongest signal banks evaluate.

Action 3: Get FICO Score Above 750

If your current FICO is below 750, spend 3-6 months optimising it before applying:

  • Bring credit utilisation below 25%
  • Pay all bills on time (auto-pay everything)
  • Avoid new credit applications
  • Dispute any errors on your credit report

A 760+ FICO with H-1B status puts you in the strongest NRI customer profile.

Action 4: Choose Banks Comfortable With H-1B

Tier 1 banks for H-1B:

  • HDFC Bank NRI — strong H-1B processing, well-trodden path
  • ICICI Bank NRI — Express Home Loan platform handles H-1B well
  • SBI — comprehensive US NRI franchise

These banks process H-1B applications routinely. Smaller banks or newer NBFCs may have less familiarity, leading to more queries.

Action 5: Use Spouse Co-Applicant if Helpful

If your spouse is also on H-1B (or H-4 with EAD) and earning, adding them as co-applicant lifts eligibility. This isn't an "H-1B risk mitigation" — it's a standard income augmentation play for any NRI couple.

Action 6: Maintain Active Indian Banking Footprint

Keep your NRE/NRO accounts active even while on H-1B:

  • Periodic remittances to NRE
  • Some India-source income (rental, dividends) routed through NRO if applicable
  • Investments through Indian platforms (mutual funds, etc.)

This builds banking relationship that translates to better service when you eventually apply for the home loan.

H-1B vs Other US Visa Categories

For completeness, how H-1B compares to other US work-authorisation categories:

H-1B (Specialty Occupation)

  • Bank treatment: Premium NRI customer
  • Loan eligibility: Standard premium rates and full eligibility
  • Notes: The benchmark for US-based NRI lending

Green Card (Permanent Resident)

  • Bank treatment: Premium NRI customer
  • Loan eligibility: Standard premium rates and full eligibility
  • Notes: Marginal advantage over H-1B in some banks; mostly equivalent treatment

O-1 Visa (Extraordinary Ability)

  • Bank treatment: Premium NRI customer
  • Loan eligibility: Same as H-1B/Green Card
  • Notes: Less common but treated similarly

L-1 (Intra-company Transfer)

  • Bank treatment: Premium NRI customer
  • Loan eligibility: Same as H-1B
  • Notes: Sometimes additional verification of US employer's relationship to applicant's prior India employer

F-1 / OPT (Student / Optional Practical Training)

  • Bank treatment: Significantly more conservative
  • Loan eligibility: Limited; often requires resident-Indian co-applicant
  • Notes: Banks see F-1/OPT as transitional status; income often modest. We'll cover this in detail in our F-1/OPT post.

H-4 / H-4 EAD (Spouse of H-1B)

  • Bank treatment: Solo H-4 (without EAD) — limited
  • H-4 with EAD: Treated similar to H-1B for income/employment verification

What I Told the San Jose Engineer

For the borrower I mentioned at the start, we contextualised:

  • His H-1B was in 6th year, with employer-sponsored Green Card processing actively underway
  • His FICO was 805
  • His US tech employer was definitely Cat-A
  • His Indian banking footprint was active (NRE account at ICICI)

The "H-1B risk" his friends had mentioned was completely misplaced for his profile. We applied to HDFC and ICICI in parallel. Both gave premium rate offers (HDFC 8.45%, ICICI 8.50%). He negotiated HDFC down to 8.35% using the parallel offer.

His ₹3.2 crore home loan in Bengaluru was sanctioned in 7 weeks (well within typical NRI timelines). The H-1B status was a non-issue at every stage — banks asked for I-797, got it, moved on.

A year later, he was using the property's rental income to support his parents in India while continuing to plan his Green Card transition in the US. The "wait until Green Card" advice he'd been considering would have meant delaying by 4-5 years and missing the lower rate environment of 2026.

Peaceful Loans's Advise

H-1B is one of the strongest visa categories for NRI home loans, not a problematic one. Banks see H-1B holders as premium NRI customers due to high income, strong employer profile, comprehensive US documentation, and predictable visa timelines.

Concerns about deportation risk, higher rates, or mandatory co-applicants are mostly myths from outdated or inaccurate sources. The actual H-1B-specific factors banks check (visa validity, employer stability, I-797, FICO) are clean and predictable for typical H-1B holders.

The few legitimate complications — recent job loss, mid-process visa transfer, expiring visa without extension — apply to any NRI with employment uncertainty, not just H-1B.

If you are on H-1B and considering an Indian home loan, don't wait for Green Card unless you have other reasons to delay. The current rate environment (April 2026 repo at 5.25%) is favorable, and the loan structure works cleanly for H-1B profiles.
