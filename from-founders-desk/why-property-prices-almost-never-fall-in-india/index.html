Why Property Prices (Almost) Never Fall in India

!Mangesh Zope

Mangesh Zope

Founder, Peaceful Loans · IIM Calcutta Alumnus

I sat down this weekend to read Vivek Kaul’s excellent two-part BasisPoint series, featuring Why Housing Prices (almost) Never Fall in India and How Expensive Homes Are Shrinking India's Future. As someone who spends all day talking to clients looking to secure big-ticket home loans (typically upwards of ₹2 Crore to ₹10 Crore), this hits incredibly close to home.

Almost every week, a client asks me: “Mangesh, these prices look completely detached from reality. Transaction volumes are falling, builders are complaining, yet prices aren't budging. Should I wait for a 20% correction?”

My answer is always a mathematical "No."

And Vivek Kaul's analysis perfectly codifies the structural reasons why. India's housing market doesn't behave like a normal market because it isn't one.

Here is my breakdown of why prices rarely correct, how developers think, and the actual math of real estate returns that builders won't show you.

1. The Owner Profile: The 1% Do Not Need to Sell

In a classic economic model, if supply exceeds demand, prices fall. But in Indian real estate, a massive share of premium housing is owned by the top 1% of the country.

These owners did not buy their properties to live in, nor did they buy them using highly leveraged home loans that require immediate rental yield to pay off. They bought them using excess capital—often recycled from IPO cashouts, promoter stake sales, corporate bonuses, or undeclared cash (black money).

Because the holding cost of real estate in India is incredibly low (negligible property taxes compared to the US or Europe), these owners can afford to keep their apartments locked and empty for decades. They have no financial pressure to discount. A correction requires forced sellers. In India's premium market, there are none.

2. Real Estate Freezes Instead of "Clearing"

In economics, a market "clears" when the price adjusts to a point where all sellers find buyers.

In India, if buyers can't afford the asking price, the market doesn't clear. It simply freezes.

Normal Market BehaviorIndian Real Estate Behavior
------
Weak demand ➔ Lower pricesWeak demand ➔ Stagnant prices + Zero transactions
Sellers cut margins to exitSellers lock the door and wait for years
Market clears quicklyMarket freezes, vacant inventory rises

When developers refuse to cut prices, they aren't being stubborn; they are managing cash flow through non-bank financing, refinancing old debt, or launching new phases at higher prices to show "paper appreciation" to their existing lenders and buyers.

3. The Controlled Narrative and the Anchoring Bias

Why does the average buyer believe property prices only ever rise? Because the narrative is completely controlled by a consolidated group of developers, brokers, and real estate consultants.

Unlike the stock market, where index values are updated every millisecond and are publicly available, India completely lacks a transparent, real-time, widely publicized real estate price index.

When transactions stagnant, brokers simply hide the data. When prices fell in parts of the National Capital Region (NCR) between 2013 and 2020, it was barely reported in mainstream media. Buyers are constantly anchored to stories of premium projects being "lapped up in 24 hours," creating artificial FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out).

4. The Real Math: The Return Builders Won't Calculate

When developers pitch real estate as a high-return investment, they usually present a very simple calculation: “Bought for ₹1 Cr in 2014, selling for ₹2.5 Cr in 2024. That's a 150% return!”

But if you run the actual Internal Rate of Return (IRR) including all hidden variables, the picture is completely different.

Let's calculate the real returns of a typical premium property investment:

Expense CategoryVariableImpact on IRR
---------
Stamp Duty & Registration5% to 8% upfrontImmediately reduces day-1 capital
Maintenance Charges₹5 to ₹15 per sq. ft. monthlyContinuous cash outflow
Property Tax & InsuranceAnnual recurring costSteady drag on net yields
Time Value of MoneyUpfront booking to possession lag (3-5 years)Massive interest/opportunity cost
Brokerage on Exit1% to 2% on sale valueReduces final payout
Capital Gains TaxLTCG (even with indexation)Substantial cut on final profit

Once you subtract these costs, a nominal 15% annual appreciation often drops to a real, net IRR of just 5% to 7%—which is lower than a risk-free fixed deposit or a simple index fund, while being completely illiquid!

5. The "Room of Requirement" Phenomenon

According to the National Real Estate Development Council (NREDC) 2024 report, over 1 crore (10 million) flats sit vacant in India.

These are what I call "Room of Requirement" properties (for the Harry Potter fans out there). They exist as pure investment tokens, locked away, while genuine home buyers struggle to find affordable housing. High NRI buying and speculative capital keep locking up supply, artificially inflating prices for end-users.

What This Means for You: A Founder's Advice

If you are looking to buy a home, do not wait for a massive market crash. It is highly unlikely to happen due to the structural factors listed above.

Instead, change your approach:

1. Buy for Consumption, Not Investment: A primary residence is a consumption asset that provides emotional security and stability. Do not try to justify it using stock-market IRR metrics.

2. Focus on Affordability-First: Do not let builder-broker narratives push you into over-leveraging. Ensure your EMI fits comfortably within your cash flows, even during job transitions.

3. Optimize the Loan Structure: If property prices won't fall, your only variable of control is your loan interest cost. Choosing between an Overdraft (OD) loan (like SBI Maxgain), structured prepayments, or negotiating the bank spread rate is where the real money is saved.
