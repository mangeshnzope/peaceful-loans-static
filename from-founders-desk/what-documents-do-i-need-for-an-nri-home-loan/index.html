What Documents Do I Need for an NRI Home Loan?

Mangesh Zope

Founder, Peaceful Loans · IIM Calcutta Alumnus

A senior consultant in London called us last quarter, slightly exasperated. He had been compiling documents for his ₹2.8 crore home loan application for nearly seven weeks. Each time he submitted what the bank requested, two more requests would surface — "Please get this notarised at the Indian Consulate," "We need apostille on this UK document," "Power of Attorney needs to be specifically drafted for this purpose."

His question to me — "Mangesh, is there a single complete list anywhere? Or am I just being asked one thing at a time?"

The honest answer is — there is a complete list, and most banks could share it on Day 1, but they often don't because their NRI documentation requirements are layered (basic + verification-specific + bank-specific + country-specific). When borrowers don't see the whole list upfront, they end up in this frustrating drip-feed loop.

This post is the complete document framework for NRI home loans in India in 2026. Build the dossier this way once, and you'll move through any major bank's process meaningfully faster.

The Five-Bucket Framework for NRI Documents

NRI home loan documents fall into five distinct buckets:

1. NRI Identity & Status — proves you are who you say you are

2. Foreign Income & Employment — proves you can repay

3. Indian Banking & Tax — proves your India-side compliance

4. Property Documents — proves the asset is clean

5. Power of Attorney & Legal Setup — proves someone in India can act for you

Resident borrowers face buckets 4 (property) and parts of 1, 2, 3. NRIs face all five, with the foreign documentation bucket being entirely additional.

Bucket 1: NRI Identity & Status Documents

The foundational identity stack:

Mandatory for All NRI Applicants

  • Indian passport (current + all renewed pages, including pages with stamps)
  • Foreign passport (if you are an OCI or have foreign citizenship)
  • OCI card (if applicable)
  • Visa pages showing current valid work permit / residence status (H-1B, L-1, Green Card, UK Tier 2, EU Blue Card, UAE residence visa, etc.)
  • PAN card (mandatory for property purchase and tax filing in India)
  • Aadhaar card (highly recommended; many banks now require it for NRI loans)

Address Proof — Both Countries

You need to demonstrate two addresses:

  • Current foreign address: utility bill, bank statement, lease agreement, or driving licence in your country of residence
  • Indian address: your Indian permanent address (often parents' home), supported by Aadhaar/voter ID/utility bill

Recent Photographs

3-4 passport-size photographs (within last 6 months). For some banks, these need to be specifically signed across the front.

Common Identity Documentation Gaps

Passport renewal mid-process — if your passport renewed during the loan application, ensure both old and new passports are submitted, with continuity demonstrated.

Visa status changes — if you converted from H-1B to Green Card, or from L-1 to L-2 to L-1 again, document the timeline cleanly.

PAN card without active status — many NRIs have inactive PAN cards (issued before going abroad, never used since). Reactivate the PAN before applying — the bank will check status.

Bucket 2: Foreign Income & Employment Documents

The largest documentation set for NRI loans, and the source of most delays:

Mandatory Income Documents

  • Last 3-6 months' salary slips from your foreign employer
  • Employment contract showing current designation, salary, joining date
  • Last 2 years' tax returns from your country of residence:
  • US: W-2 forms + Form 1040
  • UK: P60 + Self-Assessment if applicable
  • Canada: T4 slip + Income Tax Return
  • Australia: Group Certificate + Tax Return
  • UAE/GCC: salary certificate from employer (no income tax returns)
  • Singapore: IR8A + Notice of Assessment
  • Last 6 months' foreign bank statements showing salary credits and standard expenses
  • Letter from current employer confirming employment, salary, and "no objection" to home loan (some banks require this)

Variable Pay Documentation

If your compensation includes variable components:

  • Bonus history letter from HR for last 24 months
  • RSU/stock vesting schedule for next 4-8 quarters
  • Performance review letters if available
  • Equity grant letters with vesting calendar

The more "structured" your variable pay looks on paper, the more banks count of it in eligibility.

International Credit Report

This is often missed. Most major Indian banks now require:

  • US: Experian, Equifax, or TransUnion credit report
  • UK: Experian or Equifax UK credit report
  • Canada: Equifax Canada credit report
  • Australia: Equifax or Illion credit report
  • Other countries: equivalent credit bureau report

Get this 4-6 weeks before applying, since it can take 2-3 weeks to obtain in some countries.

Common Foreign Income Documentation Gaps

Old country documents: if you've moved between countries (e.g., 5 years US → 3 years UK), banks want documentation from both. Have it ready.

Translation requirements: some non-English-speaking countries (Germany, Japan, France) require certified English translations of documents. Plan 2-3 weeks lead time for this.

Apostille/notarisation: some critical documents (typically POA, but sometimes employment letters) need to be apostilled in your country and notarised at the Indian Consulate. This adds 1-3 weeks.

Bucket 3: Indian Banking & Tax Documents

The often-underestimated bucket:

NRE / NRO / FCNR Account Documentation

You need at least one of:

  • NRE (Non-Resident External) account — for foreign-earned income remitted to India
  • NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account — for India-source income (rental, dividends)
  • FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) account — foreign currency deposits

For each account: statements for last 6-12 months + account opening documents confirming NRI account status.

If you don't have these accounts set up, allocate 2-4 weeks for the bank to open them. We strongly recommend setting these up before applying for the home loan, not during.

Indian Tax Documents (If Applicable)

If you have any India-source income (rental from existing property, dividends, interest), you should be filing Indian tax returns:

  • Last 2 years' Indian ITR with computation
  • Form 26AS for the same years
  • TDS certificates if applicable

Many NRIs don't have India tax filing if they have no India-source income — that's fine, just declare it clearly.

Existing Indian Loans / Credit Profile

  • Indian CIBIL report (if you have any Indian credit history)
  • Existing Indian loan account statements (any home loan, vehicle loan, etc.)
  • Indian credit card statements (last 6 months)

If you've been abroad 10+ years with no Indian credit footprint, this is genuinely empty — explain it and rely on your international credit report.

Bucket 4: Property Documents

These are the same as for resident borrowers (we covered this in detail in our resident documentation post). Since you're not in India to coordinate, the practical mechanism differs.

For Resale Property (Most Common for NRIs)

  • Chain of title (typically 30 years)
  • Latest sale deed
  • Approved building plan
  • Occupation Certificate (OC)
  • Property tax receipts (last 3 years)
  • Society NOC for transfer and mortgage
  • Share certificate of cooperative society
  • Index II
  • Encumbrance certificate

For Under-Construction Property

  • Allotment letter and Builder-Buyer Agreement
  • RERA registration of project
  • Approved plan from local development authority
  • Commencement Certificate
  • Occupation Certificate (when ready)
  • Demand letter from builder
  • Receipts of payments already made
  • NOC from builder for mortgage

Coordination Mechanism

Most NRIs coordinate property documentation through:

  • Real estate broker (handles initial paperwork from seller/builder)
  • POA holder in India (collects original documents, deposits with bank)
  • Builder's documentation team (for under-construction)

Build a clear handoff process at the start. The bank will require originals at registration; your POA holder needs to physically handle this.

Bucket 5: Power of Attorney & Legal Setup

The bucket that NRIs typically underestimate, and which causes the most last-minute scrambles:

Power of Attorney (POA)

You need a properly drafted POA giving a trusted Indian resident the authority to:

  • Sign loan agreement and related documents on your behalf
  • Sign property registration documents
  • Coordinate with the bank for disbursement
  • Submit documents and respond to queries
  • Handle society NOCs and property tax matters

POA Drafting Specifics

The POA must explicitly mention:

  • The specific property being purchased (address, survey number, society)
  • The lender bank name (or "any bank approved by the Principal")
  • Specific authority for loan agreement, mortgage creation, registration
  • Time validity (usually 1-2 years)

A generic "general POA" often gets rejected at registration; banks and registrars want specific, property-linked POAs.

POA Execution Process

The POA must be:

1. Drafted in India (typically by your lawyer)

2. Executed by you abroad (signed in front of Indian Consulate officer or notary public)

3. Apostilled or attested at the Indian Consulate in your country of residence

4. Registered in India (at the Sub-Registrar's office in the property's jurisdiction)

This process typically takes 4-8 weeks end-to-end. Start early.

Who Should Hold the POA

The POA holder should be:

  • Resident in India (and ideally in the same city as the property)
  • Trusted personally — they will have substantial financial authority
  • Available for in-person bank/registrar visits at multiple stages

Most NRIs use a parent, sibling, or spouse. Some use a long-trusted family lawyer.

Country-Specific Documentation Variations

A few country-specific notes:

US-Based NRIs

  • W-2 + Form 1040 are standard
  • For Green Card holders, EAD documentation is sometimes asked for
  • If you have multiple state tax filings, consolidate them
  • IRS transcripts can substitute for tax return copies if originals unavailable

UK-Based NRIs

  • P60 + payslips work for most banks
  • Self-Assessment if you have rental income or other variable
  • Share scheme grant letters for any UK employer stock plans

UAE/GCC-Based NRIs

  • Salary certificate (Arabic + English) from employer
  • Emirates ID (or equivalent national ID)
  • Tenancy contract for residence proof
  • Easier overall — no income tax filings to provide

Singapore-Based NRIs

  • IR8A from employer
  • Notice of Assessment from IRAS
  • CPF statement (for completeness)

Common Documentation Mistakes That Delay Files

Five recurring patterns:

Mistake 1: Submitting Incomplete Sets

Banks process files in stages — when one document is missing, the whole file gets parked while waiting. Submit complete sets, not partial ones with "I'll send the rest later."

Mistake 2: Photocopies Without Apostille / Attestation

Critical foreign documents (especially POA, employment letters in some cases) need official attestation. Photocopies aren't accepted.

Mistake 3: Translating in India

Documents in non-English foreign languages must be translated in the country of origin by certified translators. Translations done by Indian translators are often rejected.

Mistake 4: Outdated Account Statements

Banks want statements covering the last 6 months up to within 30 days of submission. If you submit statements that are 6 weeks old, you'll be asked for fresh ones — losing 2-3 weeks of process time.

Mistake 5: Missing Co-Applicant Documentation

If you have a co-applicant (resident Indian or NRI spouse), their documentation set is fully separate from yours. Most files get delayed because co-applicant docs are incomplete.

A Pre-Submission Checklist

Before sending your dossier, verify:

✅ Passport copy + visa pages clear and current

✅ All 5 buckets of documents addressed

✅ Foreign tax returns for last 2 years complete

✅ International credit report obtained

✅ NRE/NRO accounts opened with statements

✅ Indian PAN active status confirmed

✅ Property documents organised in chain of title order

✅ POA drafted, executed, attested, and registered (or at least in process)

✅ Co-applicant documents (if applicable) complete

✅ Recent photographs ready

✅ Translations completed (if applicable)

✅ Apostille / consular attestation done where required

✅ Single indexed PDF dossier prepared

This is meaningful preparation — typically 4-6 weeks of work to assemble properly. The 4-6 weeks invested upfront save 8-12 weeks of mid-process friction.

By the time the bank had finished processing his existing documents, the POA was ready for property registration. His total elapsed time from frustration to disbursement: 11 weeks. Without the structural fix, he was tracking toward 16-20 weeks of continued drip-feed.
