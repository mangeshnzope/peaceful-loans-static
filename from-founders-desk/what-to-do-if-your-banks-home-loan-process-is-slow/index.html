What to Do If Your Bank's Home Loan Process Is Slow

Mangesh Zope

Founder, Peaceful Loans · IIM Calcutta Alumnus

A senior consultant called us last quarter, openly frustrated. He had submitted his complete home loan file to a private bank 23 days earlier. The seller's registration window was closing in 14 days. The bank's relationship manager kept saying "your file is moving" — but no concrete update, no timeline, no specific next step.

His question — "Mangesh, is there anything I can actually do to push this, or do I just have to wait and hope?"

There is plenty you can do — but most borrowers don't because they don't know where the file actually is, or who the right person to push is. The default response of "the bank is slow, I'll just be patient" is exactly how loan files end up taking 8 weeks instead of 3.

This post is the practical playbook. How to diagnose where your file is stuck, who to escalate to, and how to push the process without antagonising anyone.

First, Diagnose Where the File Actually Is

A home loan file moves through six distinct stages. Each stage has its own timeline and its own owner. When you don't know which stage you are stuck in, you don't know what to push for.

The six stages:

1. Application & document submission — file received and checked for completeness

2. Initial credit appraisal — your CIBIL pulled, FOIR computed, employer/business verified

3. Property legal verification — bank's lawyer reviews title, NOCs, RERA, etc.

4. Technical valuation — property physically inspected and valued

5. Sanction letter issuance — credit decision finalised, terms communicated

6. Disbursement — pre-disbursement conditions met, funds released

Most delays happen in stages 3 and 4, but borrowers often don't know which stage they are in. Step 1 is to find out.

How to Find Out Where Your File Is

Don't ask the executive who collected your file. They typically have visibility only into stages 1 and possibly 2. They will give you vague reassurances that don't help.

Instead, directly call the credit appraisal officer or the branch credit head assigned to your file. If you don't have their contact, ask the executive for it explicitly. They are required to share it.

Specific questions that get specific answers:

  • *"Is my file currently with credit appraisal, legal, or technical?"*
  • *"What specific document or task is pending right now?"*
  • *"What is the next concrete step that needs to happen, and who owns that step?"*
  • *"What is the realistic timeline to the next milestone?"*

If the executive cannot answer these clearly, the bank doesn't actually know where your file is — which is itself a red flag worth escalating.

The Five Common Reasons Files Stall

Across hundreds of files we have seen, delays cluster around five recurring causes. Knowing the cause changes how you push.

Cause 1: Property Documents Pending from Builder/Society/Seller

The single biggest cause of post-sanction delays. Society NOCs, builder NOCs, share certificate transfers — these come from third parties, not from the bank. The bank cannot fund without them.

What to do: Don't push the bank. Push the third party. Visit the society office in person if needed. Call the builder's documentation team directly. Banks are often blamed for delays that are actually third-party paperwork issues.

Cause 2: Property on Bank's "Not Yet Approved" List

For under-construction projects, the bank may need to do a separate "project approval" before funding individual units. This can take 2-6 weeks.

What to do: Ask explicitly whether the project is pre-approved by the bank. If not, ask for the project approval status and timeline. If the bank cannot give a clear timeline, switch to a bank where the project is already approved.

Cause 3: Underwriter Has Specific Queries

Sometimes the credit appraisal officer has a specific concern about your file — income volatility, an entry on CIBIL, mismatched documents. The officer may not have escalated it to you yet, just parked the file.

What to do: Specifically ask if there are any underwriter queries pending. If yes, address them immediately and explicitly. Don't wait for the officer to formally raise them — proactive resolution moves files faster.

Cause 4: Internal Coordination Failures

The file is technically with one team, but waiting on input from another team. The lawyer is waiting for the technical valuation. The valuation team is waiting for property access. The credit team is waiting for HR verification of your employment.

What to do: This is where escalation helps. The branch manager or cluster head can push internal coordination in ways the executive cannot.

Cause 5: Genuinely Slow Bank/Branch

Some banks and some branches are operationally slow. There is no single problem; the file just takes 6-8 weeks instead of 3-4 because that is the branch's pace.

What to do: Either accept it and plan accordingly, or move to a faster bank if your timeline cannot accommodate.

The Escalation Hierarchy

When polite check-ins are not moving the file, structured escalation works. The hierarchy (in order):

Level 1: Executive / Direct Selling Agent (DSA)

The person who initially collected your file. They have limited authority but should know basic status.

When to escalate beyond: If they don't respond within 48 hours, or give vague answers for more than a week.

Level 2: Credit Appraisal Officer

The person actually working on your file's underwriting. They have visibility into specific bottlenecks.

When to escalate beyond: If specific queries are not being raised promptly, or if the file has been "pending" without movement for 5+ working days.

Level 3: Branch Manager

Has authority over the branch's overall operations. Can push internal coordination, expedite file movement, and intervene with empanelled lawyers/valuers.

When to escalate beyond: If the branch manager is unable or unwilling to push the file forward, or if the issue is structural (e.g., the bank's policy doesn't fit your file).

Level 4: Cluster Head / Zonal Manager

Senior bank executive overseeing multiple branches. Can move files that are stuck due to branch-level constraints.

When to escalate beyond: Rare. Usually only for genuinely escalated complaints.

Level 5: Customer Care Head / Banking Ombudsman

For systemic issues — bank not responding, repeated mis-selling, regulatory violations. Most home loan delays don't reach here.

How to Escalate Without Antagonising

Bankers are people. People respond better to specific, polite, structured communication than to angry rants.

Effective Escalation Email Template

```

Subject: [Loan Application Number] - Request for Status Update and Resolution

Dear [Manager's Name],

I am writing regarding my home loan application [Application Number] submitted on [Date].

Current status of the file (as I understand it):

  • Application received: [Date]
  • Document submission complete: [Date]
  • Last update received: [Date], stating [what was communicated]

Specific concerns:

1. The file has been pending for [X working days] beyond the typical timeline indicated at the start.

2. I have a property registration deadline of [Date], which leaves limited time.

3. My specific question is [be specific - status? next step? blocker?].

Could you please:

  • Confirm the current stage of my file
  • Provide a specific timeline to next milestone
  • Identify any pending items I need to address

I appreciate your help in moving this forward.

Regards,

[Your Name]

[Phone Number]

[Email Address]

```

This format works because:

  • **It is specific.** Vague complaints get vague responses.
  • **It is polite.** Anger creates defensiveness.
  • **It asks for specific actions.** Easy to respond to.
  • **It documents the situation in writing.** Useful if you need to escalate further.

What Not to Do

Five mistakes borrowers make when files are slow:

Mistake 1: Repeatedly Calling Without New Information

Calling the same executive 5 times in 2 days saying "any update?" doesn't help. The executive knows you are anxious, but they don't have new information to share.

If there has been no update in 3-4 working days, escalate up rather than calling the same person again.

Mistake 2: Threatening to Pull the File Without Genuine Backup

"I'll go to another bank" only works if you actually have a backup option ready (another sanction letter, another bank's pre-approval). Empty threats damage your credibility.

Mistake 3: Going Through Multiple Channels Simultaneously

Calling the executive, emailing the branch manager, complaining to customer care, and escalating to social media — all at once — creates internal confusion and often slows the file further. Use one channel cleanly, escalate when needed.

Mistake 4: Hiding Issues from the Bank

If you know there is a CIBIL issue, an income mismatch, or a documentation gap — flag it proactively. Banks discover these eventually anyway. Hiding them only delays the inevitable while damaging your credibility.

Mistake 5: Skipping the Bank Entirely

Some borrowers, frustrated, just stop responding to the bank's queries while applying to another bank. This creates a "lapsed" application that may show on internal records and complicate future applications.

If you are switching banks, formally inform the original bank and request return of any retainer / processing fees per their policy.

When the Right Move Is Actually to Switch Banks

Sometimes the bank you started with is genuinely the wrong fit. Recognise these signs:

  • The bank has not approved the project (and the project doesn't get approved despite multiple weeks)
  • The bank's underwriting team has structural concerns about your profile that aren't fixable
  • The branch manager is not responsive even after escalation
  • The total elapsed time exceeds 6-8 weeks with no clear path to disbursement
  • Your registration deadline cannot be met within the bank's realistic timeline

In these cases, switching banks is the right answer — but do it right:

1. Get a quick pre-approval from the new bank before formally moving (1-2 days for clean files)

2. Communicate to the original bank that you are withdrawing the application

3. Request return of any unused fees as per bank policy

4. Don't burn bridges — you may need this bank for a future product or reference

What I Told the Senior Consultant

For the borrower I mentioned at the start, we did three things in parallel:

Action 1: Called the credit appraisal officer directly (not the executive) to identify the specific stage. The file was stuck at property legal verification — the bank's lawyer had questions about a specific society NOC.

Action 2: Visited the society office in person and got the specific NOC clarified within 48 hours.

Action 3: Wrote a structured escalation to the branch manager simultaneously, requesting a parallel push on the technical valuation so we could compress the remaining timeline.

Total elapsed time from frustration to disbursement: 12 days. The seller's registration deadline was met. No personality conflict, no antagonism — just structured pressure on the right people.

The lesson: most slow files don't need a bigger push. They need a more targeted one.

Peaceful Loans's Advise

A slow home loan process is a problem with multiple specific causes — third-party documentation, project approval, underwriter queries, internal coordination, or structural slowness at the bank. Each cause has a different remedy.

The first action is always diagnosis: where is the file actually stuck and why? Specific, polite communication with the right person at the bank moves files in ways generic phone calls don't.

If the situation is genuinely structural (wrong bank for your profile, project not approved, etc.), recognise it early and switch — don't waste 8 weeks hoping the bank will figure it out.
